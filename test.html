The sturdy wood of the rifle held firm in the palm of my hand, smoothed yet worn. It’s feel was familiar although distant, like the aroma of mothers cooking, something vague and lost to the winding corridors and twisting caverns of my mind. The memory came back in waves, a hunting trip, a deer, tears and death. I remembered how avoidant I was of the rifle, how father pushed and pushed me to try it. I remembered how he never took no as an answer from anybody. Not from mother, not from the women at bars and truck stops, and certainly not from me. I felt the gun against my shoulder as I had back then, how my weak arms could barely hold it, how it bruised my arm and face when i shot the deer in the leg. How I cried as it writhed and squirmed in pain as it let out its dying breaths. It was the only time i felt my father was proud for me, even though he never said it. but I could feel it from the rotten grin of satisfaction on his face as he looked at me, covered in bruises both new and old with tears running down my cheeks. But father’s temper always wrangled control of him when I expressed any emotions he felt unsavory to.
